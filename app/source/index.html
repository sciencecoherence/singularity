    What the four numbers are

    Planetary K index — how hard the Earth’s magnetic field is shaking, on a scale of 0 to 9. Under 3 is a normal day. 5 is where NOAA starts calling it a storm. 9 has happened a handful of times in living memory.

    Solar wind — the sun is always blowing a thin gas past us. 300 to 500 km/s is ordinary. Above 650 usually means a storm arrives within a day or so.

    X-ray flux class — what the sun is doing at this moment. The letter is a power of ten, so each step is ten times the last: A and B are the background hum, C is small, M is medium, X is large. Only M and X do anything we notice, and what they do is fade shortwave radio on the daylight side of the planet for a few minutes.

    Solar flux, F10.7 — the background loudness of the sun’s eleven-year cycle, measured as radio brightness at 10.7 cm. It says what kind of year it is rather than what kind of day. It also sets how puffed-up the upper atmosphere is, which is what decides how much friction a low satellite feels.

    Four readings of one field, published separately by NOAA and read separately here, so one failure does not blank the others.
